Supply chain operations

The orders that arrive as an email and a PDF.

EDI covers the partners who set it up. Everything else arrives as a PDF on an email, a changed date buried in a reply, or a short shipment someone notices at the dock. A person reads it and types it in. That reading is where we build.

All industries
01

Order intake

Highest volume

Purchase orders arrive in whatever format each customer happens to use. The agent reads them, checks them against your price lists and stock, and creates the sales order. Anything that doesn't match goes to a person with the reason.

  • PO lines read from a PDF, an email body or a spreadsheet, whatever the layout
  • Prices and part numbers checked against your master data before entry
  • Order created in the ERP, or held with the mismatch named
02

Supplier confirmations

Highest volume

You send a PO. The supplier confirms a different date, quantity or price, somewhere in an email thread. The agent reads the acknowledgement, compares it with what you ordered, and tells the planner what changed.

  • Acknowledgements reconciled line by line against the PO
  • Date, quantity and price changes flagged to the buyer who owns the order
  • Missing confirmations chased before they turn into late deliveries
03

Delay notices

Strong fit

A carrier delay or a supplier slip lands in a shared inbox. By the time someone reads it, the planner has lost a day. The agent reads it, works out which orders it touches, and tells the right person.

  • Delay notices matched to the open orders and customers they affect
  • Impact stated plainly: which order, how late, which customer
  • Expedite options prepared for a person to approve
04

Receiving discrepancies

Strong fit

Short shipments, damaged goods, wrong items. A claim needs the PO, the packing slip, the receipt and the photos, and they sit in four places. The agent puts them together.

  • Received quantities compared with the PO and the advance ship notice
  • Supplier claims drafted with every document attached
  • Credit notes tracked until they actually arrive
05

Master data upkeep

Approval only

Lead times, item attributes and supplier prices drift out of date. The agent finds the drift and proposes the change. A person approves anything that touches master data.

  • Quoted lead times compared with what suppliers actually delivered
  • Proposed updates queued for a person, with the evidence attached
01 How the agent works

Same pattern on every message. Read it, match it, act inside your rules, escalate the rest.

1 A message arrives

An order, a confirmation, a delay notice. By email, portal or scan, in whatever format the sender chose.

2 It reads and matches

The agent ties it to the open order, the item master and the customer, across every system it lives in.

3 It acts inside your rules

When everything matches your tolerances, it creates the order or updates the date. Nothing else.

4 Exceptions go to a person

What changed, what it affects, and a proposed next step. The planner decides.

Where we say no

The agent never changes master data, commits to a customer delivery date, or places a purchase order on its own. It prepares those with the evidence, and a person approves. If most of your volume already runs through EDI, an agent has little to add there, and we'll tell you so.
