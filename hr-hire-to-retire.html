HR operations

Hire to retire, without the queue.

Every stage of the employee lifecycle generates the same kind of work: a form to fill, a system to update, a checklist to chase. The agent does that part. Every decision about a person stays with a person.

All industries
One spine, five stages, four systems
01

Hire

Strong fit

Requisition to offer is mostly scheduling and paperwork wrapped around a small number of real decisions. The agent handles the wrapper. The interview panel makes the call.

  • Requisition approvals routed and status tracked without a follow-up email
  • Interview scheduling handled across calendars, panels rebooked on conflict
  • Offer letters generated from the approved terms and background checks tracked to close
02

Onboard

Highest volume

A new hire's first weeks touch a dozen systems that don't talk to each other. The agent gets every one of them updated on day one, not week three.

  • Identity, payroll and access accounts provisioned from one approved record
  • New-hire documents collected, checked, and chased when something is missing
  • Day-one checklist run against every team, not just HR's part of it
03

Core HR changes

Highest volume

Transfers, promotions, a changed bank detail, a new manager. Small events that fan out into every HR and payroll system separately. The agent keeps them in sync.

  • Approved changes propagated across HRIS, payroll and access systems together
  • Mismatched records between systems flagged before they cause a payroll error
04

Payroll & benefits

Evidence work only

Payroll runs on data assembled from timesheets, attendance and benefit elections. The agent checks it before the cutoff, so the exceptions are found with time to fix them.

  • Missing timesheets and mismatched bank details flagged before the payroll run
  • Benefits enrollment processed against the plan rules, exceptions routed to a person
05

Offboard & retire

Strong fit

An exit has a checklist as long as onboarding, and it usually gets rushed. The agent runs it the same way every time, whoever is leaving and whatever the reason.

  • Access revocation requested across every system on the last working day, not after
  • Final settlement data assembled from attendance, leave balance and dues
01 How the agent works

Same pattern at every stage. The agent prepares. A person decides on the person.

1 The event happens

A requisition is approved, a new hire signs, a transfer is confirmed. Something changed and it needs to land everywhere it matters.

2 The agent checks policy

Approval chains, eligibility rules, plan terms. The paperwork gets built against what your policy actually says.

3 It prepares the action

The letter, the system update, the checklist item, staged and ready with the reasoning attached.

4 A person approves

Every system updates together once approved. Nothing about a person's employment moves on its own.

Where we say no

We don't build anything that scores, ranks, or decides on a candidate or an employee. No hiring calls, no performance ratings, no compensation decisions, no disciplinary or termination judgement. The agent prepares the record and the paperwork. The decision about a person is never one of its outputs.
